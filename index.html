Three layers, one clear alert

In a busy newborn ward, one nurse watches many cribs at once. Each layer answers one simple question so every alert is easy to trust.

01 · DETECT

Is it a cry?

YAMNet listens every half second and separates newborn cries from ward voices, monitor beeps, and quiet.

No training needed · AUC ≈ 0.97
02 · TRIAGE

How urgent?

Two models vote — YAMNet and our custom CNN — to rate distress as urgent (pain, fear) or not urgent (needs).

Balanced accuracy ≈ 85% · Locked test
03 · CONTEXT

What to check first?

Nurse tap logs track time since last feed and diaper change to suggest the most likely care step.

Adjustable per baby · Clear rules
SAFETY RULES Every cry alerts the nurse AI unsure → treated as urgent Long crying turns red

HONEST NOTES
  • Small test set: true accuracy may vary by a few percent, and the 88% re-check stays secondary to the official 85% score.
  • Cross-validation across all data reached 84% (~80% within each single source), with near-duplicates strictly isolated by split.
  • Next milestone: collect a fresh 50–100 cry test set before reporting updated metrics.
WHY URGENCY, NOT TRANSLATION

A 2024 study showed neither humans nor AI can reliably identify why a baby cries from sound alone — but acoustics do reflect distress. NeoGuardian rates urgency from sound and pairs it with feed and diaper times.

Lockhart-Baron et al., Communications Psychology, 2024
