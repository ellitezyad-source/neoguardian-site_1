THE PROBLEM

In a newborn ward, one nurse may look after many babies at the same time. A cry at the far end of the room can wait too long — and not every cry needs the same speed.

OUR IDEA

NeoGuardian listens for the nurse. It hears a cry, rates how urgent it is, and sends one clear alert to her phone with what to check first.

Three layers, one alert

Each layer answers one simple question, so nurses can trust and understand every alert.

LAYER 1

Is it a cry?

A pretrained sound model (YAMNet) listens every half second and separates baby cries from talking, alarms and silence.

No training needed · AUC ≈ 0.97
LAYER 2

How urgent?

Two models vote — YAMNet and our own CNN — to rate the cry as urgent (pain, fear) or not urgent (needs).

Balanced accuracy ≈ 85% · locked test
LAYER 3

What to check first?

Clear rules use the nurse's taps — time since the last feed and diaper change — to suggest a likely reason.

Every setting adjustable per baby

HONEST NOTES
  • The test set is small, so the real value may be a few percent higher or lower.
  • The 88% number is a second look at the same test files, after we changed the model and chose its threshold on validation files. It is not a clean test, so the official result stays 85%.
  • Inside each single data source, balanced accuracy is about 80%. With 5-fold cross-validation on all data it is 84%.
  • Next step: a new clean test set of 50–100 cries before we claim any new number.
  • Near-duplicate recordings were found and kept in the same split, so the test never saw copies.
WHY URGENCY, NOT TRANSLATION

A 2024 study found that neither people nor AI could reliably tell why a baby cries from the sound alone — but a cry does show how distressed the baby is. So NeoGuardian rates urgency, and uses feed and diaper times for the likely reason.

Lockhart-Baron et al., Communications Psychology, 2024
